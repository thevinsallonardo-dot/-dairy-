01 — The Herds

Two animals. One hillside. Strong opinions.

The Pahari cow gives you patience in a bottle. The yak gives you altitude in a bottle. Neither of them respects a schedule.

The Pahari Cow

Indigenous · Low Yield · High Integrity

The small hill cattle of Kinnaur and the surrounding districts. Modest milk, dense fat, and a temperament that has survived several hundred winters without complaint. We do not push her. We wait for her.

Paneer

The Everyday Hero

Paneer

Fresh Kinner or Yak milk, set with lemon, cut by hand. No fillers, no shortcuts, no "paneer-style" anything.

Firm, dense, and it holds its shape under pressure. We're not going to elaborate.

Dahi

The Culture

Dahi

Set in clay pots, the old way. Live cultures, thick curd, a tang that remembers the hills. The pot sweats; the curd sets.

Thick enough to stand a spoon in. We're just going to leave that there.

Ghee

The Gold

Bilona Ghee

Churned from cultured Pahari cow butter in small batches. Nutty, granular, and the reason your grandmother's rotis were better than yours.

So rich it should come with a warning label and a lie-down.

🥣

Makkhan

White Butter

Makkhan

Hand-churned white butter, unsalted and unbothered. The base of our ghee, and the best thing to happen to hot parathas since forever.

Soft, spreadable, and gone before the parathas are. Make of that what you will.

🍥

Khoa / Mawa

For the Sweets

Khoa

Slow-reduced milk solids, stirred until the water gives up. The starting point of every good mithai in this country.

Buy it "for a recipe" and you will eat half of it standing at the counter. We all do.

🥤

Lassi

The Cool Down

Lassi

Churned dahi, cold water, a little salt or a little sugar — and no permission to add ice cream, no matter what the city does.

Namkeen or meethi. Or "another one," which is how it usually goes.

🪨

Churpi

The Hard Cheese

Yak Churpi

Smoked, dried, and hard enough to be considered a structural material. Chew it for an hour. It will outlast your patience.

Not a snack. A commitment. Do not bite it with your front teeth; we cannot help you.

🍨

Shrikhand

Sweet & Strained

Shrikhand

Dahi hung overnight until it stops dripping, then beaten with sugar, saffron and cardamom. Thick enough to hold a spoon upright.

Technically a dessert. Practically a personality trait.

03 — The Process

Four steps. No shortcuts. No "milk solids."

01

Milking

Hand-milked, morning and evening, by people who know each animal by name. The herd sets the schedule — not a delivery window.

02

Churning

Cultured overnight, then bilona-churned for butter and ghee. Slow, manual, and deeply inefficient. That is the point. Yes, we know what "bilona" sounds like. We are not explaining it.

03

Setting

Dahi set in clay. Paneer cut by hand. Churpi smoked and dried for days. Nothing is accelerated with a machine or a shortcut.

04

Packing

Glass, paper and clay wherever we can manage it. Chilled and dispatched the same day. If it cannot survive the trip, we do not send it.
